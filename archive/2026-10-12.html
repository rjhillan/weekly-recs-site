A weekly letter Weekly Recs October 12, 2026 · Autumn
"Rooms You Used to Know"

Been thinking about how certain things feel like walking into a space you haven't visited in years. The furniture's different but the light hits the same way. Everything this week has that quality of almost-recognition.

Vilhelm Hammershøi
Interior with Woman at Piano, Strandgade 30
Vilhelm Hammershøi · Interior with Woman at Piano, Strandgade 30
Oil on canvas · Danish Symbolism, 1901 · Art Institute of Chicago

A woman in a grey dress sits at a piano in an empty room. All muted tones, all stillness. Hammershøi painted the same Copenhagen apartment over and over, finding infinite variations in quietness.

Listen
Immunity New
Clairo · 2019 · Start with "Bags"

Soft-focus bedroom pop that grew up without losing its intimacy. The production sits between lo-fi and polished in a way that never settles, like Oklou if she leaned into restraint. Gets under your skin quietly.

Watch
After Life
Dir. Hirokazu Kore-eda · 1998

People in a way-station after death choose one memory to keep forever. Kore-eda films it like a documentary, interviewing real people about their actual memories alongside actors. Gentle until it destroys you.

Read
Die Ringe des Saturn
W. G. Sebald · 1995

A walk along the Suffolk coast that spirals into everything else: silkworms, Thomas Browne, the bombing of Germany. Sebald moves between subjects the way memory actually works, not in chapters but in associations.

Words
In a Station of the Metro
Ezra Pound · 1913
The apparition of these faces in the crowd: Petals on a wet, black bough.

Two lines that do what this whole week is about: the shock of seeing something familiar made strange.

How it all connects

The Hammershøi painting looks like how the quietest parts of Immunity sound. Kore-eda's film does what Sebald does in prose: lets one thing lead to another without forcing the connection. And that Pound poem is the whole thing in miniature, the way a single image can crack open into something larger. They all trust stillness to do heavy lifting.

Something to Read
On Staying the Same Person
Merve Emre · The New Yorker

Emre writes about personality tests and the fantasy that we're consistent across time. She's skeptical and funny about the industries built on this idea while taking seriously what we want from self-knowledge.
