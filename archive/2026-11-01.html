A weekly letter Weekly Recs November 01, 2026 · Autumn
"Things That Hold Their Shape"

Walked past a building site this week and watched them pour concrete into forms. Got me thinking about all the invisible structures that keep things from collapsing. The routines, the repetitions, the containers we barely notice until they're gone.

Giorgio Morandi
Still Life
Giorgio Morandi · Still Life
Oil on canvas · 1956 · Wikimedia Commons

Morandi painted the same bottles and jars for decades. Different arrangements, same objects. The colours are dusty, muted, barely there. You start seeing the air between things as much as the things themselves.

Listen
Crusher New
Crumb · 2019 · Start with "Ghostride"

Psychedelic bedroom pop that moves like smoke through a room. Lila Ramani's voice floats over these warped, wobbly guitar lines and jazz-touched drums. Feels late-night and loose but never falls apart.

Watch
The Gleaners and I
Dir. Agnès Varda · 2000

Varda films people who collect what others throw away—food, objects, meaning. She's in her seventies with a handheld camera, finding shape in leftovers. Generous and curious without being precious about it.

Read
Dept of Speculation
Jenny Offill · 2014

A marriage in fragments. Each page is a paragraph or two, sometimes just a line. Reads fast but stays with you. Offill knows how to hold a whole life in the gaps between moments.

Words
The Orange
Wendy Cope · 1986
At lunchtime I bought a huge orange— The size of it made us all laugh. I peeled it and shared it with Robert and Dave— They got quarters and I had a half. And that orange, it made me so happy, As ordinary things often do Just lately. The shopping. A walk in the park. This is peace and contentment. It's new. The rest of the day was quite easy. I did all the jobs on my list And enjoyed them and had some time over. I love you. I'm glad I exist.

Cope makes a whole philosophy out of an orange at lunch. The structure holds something small until it feels huge.

How it all connects

Morandi's bottles are like the forms in that concrete pour—containers that give shape to empty space. Crumb's songs and Offill's fragments both trust repetition to hold things together without explaining too much. Varda and Didion both collect small moments and let the structure reveal itself later. Cope's orange is the plainest version of all this: something ordinary you can hold in your hand.

Something to Read
On Keeping a Notebook
Joan Didion · Slouching Towards Bethlehem

Didion writes about why she saves scraps of observation that don't mean anything to anyone else. It's about building a container for memory, even when memory lies. Sharp and honest about the self-deception involved in writing anything down.
