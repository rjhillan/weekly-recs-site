A weekly letter Weekly Recs October 25, 2026 · Autumn
"Things That Hold Their Shape"

Been thinking about structure this week. Not rigid scaffolding, but the way certain things maintain form even when everything around them shifts. The album that sounds chaotic but never loses the thread. The painting held together by a single repeated curve.

Hilma af Klint
The Swan, No. 17
Hilma af Klint · The Swan, No. 17
Oil on canvas · Abstract, 1915 · Art Institute of Chicago

Two swans, one black, one white, their necks forming a perfect heart. Painted before anyone was supposedly doing abstraction. The geometry feels inevitable, like she found a shape that was already there waiting. Hangs in the Guggenheim but was hidden for decades.

Listen
Romantic Piano New
Kai Whiston · 2020 · Start with "No Best Friend"

The title is a joke and dead serious at once. Whiston builds these intricate electronic structures that collapse and rebuild themselves every few bars, but there's always a melody holding the centre. Feels like watching something break in slow motion and realizing it was meant to.

Watch
In the Mood for Love
Dir. Wong Kar-wai · 2000

Two neighbours realize their spouses are having an affair with each other. The whole film is structured around repetition—same corridors, same noodle shop, same dresses—but each repetition shifts the meaning slightly. Everything held in place by formality and colour.

Read
Deaf Republic
Ilya Kaminsky · 2019

A town goes deaf when soldiers shoot a boy. The poems are spare, almost blunt, but they're held together by this fictional town that feels realer than memory. Kaminsky keeps the structure tight even when describing chaos.

Words
The Red Wheelbarrow
William Carlos Williams · 1923
so much depends upon a red wheel barrow glazed with rain water beside the white chickens.

Nine words doing the work of a hundred. The line breaks make you slow down and see the shape of each image.

How it all connects

Whiston's tracks fold in on themselves the way af Klint's swans mirror each other, both finding patterns that feel discovered rather than imposed. Williams breaks 'wheelbarrow' across two lines the same way Wong Kar-wai repeats that hallway—the structure creates the meaning. Kaminsky's town and Didion's notebooks both understand that form is what lets you hold difficult truths without letting them scatter.

Something to Read
On Keeping a Notebook
Joan Didion · Slouching Towards Bethlehem

Didion writing about why she writes things down, what notebooks preserve and what they can't. She's interested in the structure of memory—how certain details become load-bearing while others disappear. First published in Holiday magazine, later collected in her 1968 essay collection.
