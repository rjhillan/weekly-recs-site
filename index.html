A weekly letter Weekly Recs March 02, 2026 · Spring
"Instructions for Looking Sideways"

Been thinking about how some things only make sense if you don't look straight at them. Peripheral vision, glances caught in mirrors, the way certain sounds clarify when you turn your head slightly. Everything this week rewards the indirect approach.

Philip Guston · The Street
Oil on canvas · Late period, 1977 · Art Institute of Chicago

Pink cartoon figures in pointed hoods driving a car full of disembodied legs. Looks like a comic strip until you realize what you're actually looking at. Guston painted these after abandoning abstract expressionism and everyone hated them at first.

Listen
Immunity
Clairo · 2019 · Start with "Bags"

Feels like overhearing someone working through something real in another room. The production is warmer than you expect, guitars sit next to drum machines like they've always belonged together. Gets better the quieter you play it.

Watch
Mulholland Drive
Dir. David Lynch · 2001

The plot dissolves if you try to follow it straight through, but it holds together perfectly if you let it wash over you sideways. Naomi Watts does two different versions of the same person, or maybe the same version of two people. Makes more sense at 2am.

Read
Remainder
Tom McCarthy · 2007

A man gets a settlement after an accident he can't remember and spends it recreating moments that feel real by making them absolutely perfect. Gets stranger and more obsessive as it goes. The sentences are clean and flat in a way that makes the weirdness hit harder.

Words
The Red Wheelbarrow
William Carlos Williams · 1923
so much depends upon a red wheel barrow glazed with rain water beside the white chickens.

Everything important is in how you break the lines, where your eye has to pause and shift.

How it all connects

Guston's hooded figures are cartoons that stop being funny the moment you see them clearly, same way Mulholland Drive only coheres when you stop trying to solve it. The wheelbarrow poem does everything through what it doesn't say. Clairo's album sounds like bedroom pop until you notice the studio craft holding it all up, just out of frame. McCarthy's narrator can only feel real by building elaborate fakes.

Something to Read
A Rape on Campus
Sabrina Rubin Erdely · Rolling Stone

A notorious piece of journalism that fell apart under scrutiny, later retracted. Including it not for what it claimed but as an example of how narrative can bend truth when you want a story to fit too badly. Worth reading about rather than reading directly.
